Method

  • MetricDeaths per year, worldwide unless the entry says otherwise. Lower is better.
  • ScoringIf the source reports an annual estimate, that estimate is the score. If it reports a count over a period, the score is the count divided by the length of the period. Each row's detail shows the arithmetic.
  • SourcesOne primary source per entry, preferring WHO and UN statistics, then peer-reviewed estimates, then institutional estimates, then curated trackers.
  • UncertaintyWhere the source gives an interval, or published estimates disagree, the row shows the range. An entry with a range and no central estimate is ranked by the geometric midpoint.
  • AI attributionA death is confirmed when a court, coroner, regulator or accident investigator has found that a machine-learning system caused or contributed to it. It is alleged when a named system is blamed in a lawsuit, an open investigation, or reporting by at least two news organisations. These are the primary tracker's definitions. The AI score counts both, for the latest complete year.
  • Comparison entriesBoeing MCAS and industrial robots are conventional automation, not machine learning. They do not count towards the AI score.

Limits

  • RetrospectiveKillBench scores recorded deaths, not risk. Much of the concern about advanced AI is about future harm: in a 2023 survey of 2,778 AI researchers, the median respondent gave a 5% chance of outcomes as bad as human extinction. A low score here is not evidence about that.
  • Incomplete AI dataTrackers depend on lawsuits and news reports. Driver-assistance deaths in 2025 are missing, deaths nobody has linked to a system are not counted, and alleged cases may not hold up.
  • Military useNot scored. Reporting says an AI system called Lavender marked up to 37,000 people in Gaza as targets. The Israeli military disputes the account, and no source attributes a number of deaths to the software.
  • Unequal scopeKnives counts homicides only, Malaria excludes other mosquito-borne diseases, and Industrial robots covers one country.
  • AttributionDeaths attributed to a ruler depend on what is counted and whose estimate is used, which is why those ranges are wide.
  • PopulationRates are not adjusted for world population, which has more than tripled since 1950.
  • BenefitEntries are scored on deaths caused, with no credit for deaths prevented.
