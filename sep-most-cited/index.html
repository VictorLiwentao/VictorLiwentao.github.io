        How the counts were made

        The rules follow Eric Schwitzgebel and Jordan Jackson’s published method as closely as possible, so the two snapshots can be compared.

        Source

        Works

        Citations match when the first author matches and the titles begin with the same words, ignoring punctuation, capitals and a leading “a”, “an” or “the”. Editions, reprints, near-identical spellings and multi-volume works are merged; translations are not. Editor citations are not counted. Only works first published in 1900 or later are ranked, so Sidgwick’s The Methods of Ethics (1874) is left out; Schwitzgebel’s list included it under its 1907 edition, and it would tie for 10th here.

        Philosophers

        Name variants are merged. People who share a surname and initial are separated by first name, middle initial and, where needed, the titles they are cited for (Paul and Patricia Churchland, Charles and C. C. W. Taylor, J. S. and J. L. Bell). Three name keys that mix several people too thoroughly to separate (J. Allen, M. Martin and S. Smith) are left out. The main list covers authors born in 1900 or later; ancient and medieval authors are omitted from the wider view because SEP usually cites them by title alone.

        Fields and the map

        Entries were clustered by the authors they cite (k-means on their citation profiles), and the clusters were named by hand and grouped into eight fields; a few entries land in a neighbouring field. A philosopher’s colour is the field with most of their citing entries. The map is a UMAP projection of each philosopher’s citing entries, weighted so that entries citing many listed people count for less.

        Accuracy

        Spot checks against a raw-text search of every bibliography put most counts within one to three entries. Small changes since 2024 can come from differences between two independent scrapers as well as real revisions. Corrections are welcome.
