Rawls and David Lewis still top the Stanford Encyclopedia’s citations

John Rawls’s A Theory of Justice is cited in more entries of the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy than any other work published since 1900, and David Lewis is the most-cited philosopher born since 1900. These counts update Eric Schwitzgebel and Jordan Jackson’s 2024 lists, rebuilt from every current entry.

      How the counts were made

      The rules follow Schwitzgebel’s published method as closely as possible, so the two snapshots can be compared.

      Source

      Works

      Citations are matched to the same work when the author matches and the titles begin with the same words, ignoring punctuation, capitals and a leading “a”, “an” or “the”. Later editions, reprints and near-identical spellings are merged; multi-volume works are merged; translations are not, so a work cited in both English and its original language is split. Citations in the role of editor are not counted. Co-authored works are listed under their first author.

      Philosophers

      Name variants (initials, middle names, nicknames, misspellings) are merged. People who share a surname and initial are separated by first name, middle initial and, where needed, by the titles they are cited for: for example Paul and Patricia Churchland, Charles Taylor and C. C. W. Taylor, J. S. Bell and J. L. Bell. The main list covers authors born in 1900 or later, as Schwitzgebel’s does. The toggle adds earlier authors, but ancient and medieval authors are left out because SEP usually cites their works by title alone, so a name-based count would mean little.

      Accuracy

      Spot checks against a raw-text search of every bibliography put most counts within one to three entries of the truth. Small changes since 2024 can come from differences between two independent scrapers, as well as real revisions, so treat a gain of a few entries as noise. Corrections are welcome.
